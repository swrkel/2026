MA-002 - PetroPD settlement store() : STRUCTURAL MAP
=====================================================================
Written 5 Aug 2026, against Modules/PetroPD/Http/Controllers/Settlement/
Concerns/CreatesPdSettlements.php after the file split.

PURPOSE
  store() is 3,271 lines. Before anyone tries to break it up, this is
  what it actually does and in what order. No code was changed to
  produce this document.

  I am deliberately NOT refactoring it yet. The point of writing this
  first is that a refactor of a method that posts money should start
  from a map, not from a guess about what the method is for.


=====================================================================
THE THREE PHASES
=====================================================================
    lines    1 -  393      393 lines   validation and preparation
    lines  394 - 2769     2375 lines   inside DB::beginTransaction()
    lines 2770 - 3271      502 lines   AFTER DB::commit()

  DB::beginTransaction()  at line 394
  DB::commit()            at line 2769
  DB::rollBack()          at line 3206, in the catch


PHASE 1 - lines 1-393, before the transaction
  Validation and shape-checking, with SIX early exits returning
  response()->json(). Also several small closures computing line
  subtotals and discounts, which is why the line count is high for what
  is conceptually just "check the request".

  Nothing is written here. Safe to extract - this is the least risky part
  of the whole method and the obvious first target.

  Reading that list, the phase has five natural stages:

     C  payments, cash reconciliation, ledger        1054 - 1726
     D  credit sales and receivable posting          1726 - 2013
     E  shortage and excess accounts                 2013 - 2085

  Those are candidate extraction boundaries. They are NOT proven safe -
  see the caution at the end.


=====================================================================
PHASE 3 - AFTER THE COMMIT. THIS IS THE FINDING THAT MATTERS.
=====================================================================
Two operations that WRITE TO THE DATABASE run after DB::commit():

   2796   is1497CleanupDuplicateSettlementPostings   105 lines
              performs DELETE and UPDATE

   2820   ensureSettlementCardAccounting             165 lines
              performs CREATE and UPDATE

  Everything else after the commit is read-only or presentational -
  notifyPetroPdSettlementSaved, getFilteredCreditSalePayments,
  hydrateSettlementPdMeterSalesForDisplay, buildSettlementViewLookups,
  the WhatsApp redirect at 3037 and the print view at 3192.

WHY IT MATTERS
  Those two writes are outside the transaction, so they are not covered
  by the rollBack at line 3206. If either fails - a lock timeout, a
  constraint, a PHP error - the settlement is ALREADY COMMITTED and:

    - duplicate settlement postings that the cleanup was meant to remove
      stay in the ledger, or
    - card accounting rows are missing or half-written

  and nothing reverses the settlement. The user sees an error, the
  settlement exists, and the books are inconsistent in a way no retry
  fixes, because a retry hits the duplicate guard.

  Note also that ensureSettlementCardAccounting is called TWICE - once
  inside the transaction at 1726 and again after the commit at 2820.
  Whether the second call is a deliberate safety net or a leftover is
  worth establishing; if it is a safety net, it is one that runs where a
  failure cannot be undone.

WHAT I HAVE NOT DONE
  I have not moved those two calls inside the transaction. It looks like
  the right change, but "looks like" is not enough here:

    - the cleanup may deliberately run after commit because it needs to
      see committed rows, in which case moving it changes what it finds
    - moving writes into a transaction lengthens it, and this transaction
      is already 2,375 lines of work; on a busy tenant that is more lock
      contention

  Both are answerable, but by reading those two methods properly and
  testing on a real settlement - not by me assuming from a call-site map.


=====================================================================
SUGGESTED ORDER OF WORK, LOWEST RISK FIRST
=====================================================================
  1. Extract PHASE 1 (validation, 393 lines) into its own method or a
     form-request class. Nothing is written there, so behaviour cannot
     change. Immediate 393-line reduction.

  2. Establish the truth about the two post-commit writes. Read both
     methods, decide whether they belong inside the transaction, and test
     on a real settlement. This is a correctness question, not a tidying
     one, and it is the most valuable item on this list.

  3. Extract PHASE 3's read-only and presentational tail (the response
     building, print view, WhatsApp redirect) - roughly 400 lines once
     the two writes are resolved. Low risk, purely output.

  4. Only then consider splitting PHASE 2 along the A-E boundaries
     above, one stage per change, each with a before/after comparison of
     the rows written for the same settlement input.

  Steps 1 and 3 together take store() below 2,500 lines without touching
  anything that writes. Step 4 is where the real risk lives and it should
  be last, with the map above as the starting point rather than a fresh
  reading each time.


=====================================================================
HOW TO CHECK ANY CHANGE TO THIS METHOD
=====================================================================
  Post one settlement on a test tenant, then capture every row it wrote:

    SELECT * FROM transactions          WHERE petro_settlement_id = ?;
    SELECT * FROM account_transactions  WHERE transaction_id IN (...);
    SELECT * FROM contact_ledgers       WHERE ...;
    SELECT * FROM transaction_payments  WHERE ...;

  Make the change, post an identical settlement, and diff the two sets
  ignoring ids and timestamps. Same rows, same amounts, same accounts, or
  the change is wrong. That comparison is the only thing that makes a
  refactor of this method safe, and it is worth automating before step 4
  rather than during it.
